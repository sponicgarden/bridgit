BRIDGIT / HOW IT WORKS

Your agent.
Your phone.
A new possibility.

Bridgit turns your Android into a device your AI agent can use—and an appliance you can make your own.

The complete experience we’re building for release brings device tools, agent-created workflows and owner controls together in one product.

The sample screens below show Bridgit 1.5.4. Private connection details are withheld; the workflow descriptions also cover the experience planned for release.

1. Connect your favorite agent

Install Bridgit on a phone you own and connect it to your private network. Add a named agent, then copy its pairing prompt into a compatible assistant or agent harness. The prompt gives it the connection details and its own key.

Use the documented API or MCP with an agent that can reach your phone. A hosted assistant can connect through a suitable connector. You can rotate a key or remove an agent whenever you choose.

2. Choose what the phone can do

Enable the capabilities you want to use: camera, microphone, screen, photos, location, files, messages, audio playback, sensors and device controls. Grant the corresponding Android permissions on the phone.

Give each agent the access its job needs. Review permissions on the device and change them as your needs change.

3. Describe the job

Ask your agent for a simple action, or give the phone a bigger purpose. “Show me what is on the workbench.” “Read the next recipe step aloud.” “Turn this spare phone into a family message board.”

Your agent supplies the reasoning and assembles the workflow. Bridgit supplies the device capabilities, an interface on the phone and a local runtime for approved actions.

4. Review and activate

Before a workflow runs, review what it will do, which permissions it needs, where information will go and how long it should operate. Approve it on the phone, then activate it.

Approved local actions run on the device. Tasks that use a remote agent, send a message or reach another service still need the appropriate connection. Power, connectivity and Android restrictions affect availability.

5. Stay in control

See which appliance or session is active, review requests and results in Activity, and pause or stop the connection from the phone. Revoke an agent’s access when its job is done. An agent cannot grant itself more access or override your stop.

Bridgit also gives agents a way to reach you: notices, questions, choices, interactive cards and live text. Your answer can be the next step in the workflow.

One phone. Many useful jobs.

A scanner, a voice notebook, a project diary, a language-practice companion or a window into your agent’s work. Start with an idea, adapt it with your agent and keep the controls in your hands.
